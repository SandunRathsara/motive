# Food recognition and nutrition grounding options

Research date: **1 November 2026**. Resolves [research issue #3](https://github.com/SandunRathsara/motive/issues/3); informs, but does not decide, [Phase 1 roadmap #1](https://github.com/SandunRathsara/motive/issues/1).

## Scope and evidence rules

The controlling brief is a **personal-use iPhone app**, with **photo and one-sentence natural-language text only**. Both create editable **Food Drafts**, captured offline and recognized later through a stateless server proxy. Barcode, saved meals, and manual-macro entry are not Phase 1 fallback paths. The older prototype requirements and competitive report in this worktree describe broader entry modes; this research follows the narrower ticket brief instead and does not change those documents. Context: [ticket](https://github.com/SandunRathsara/motive/issues/3), [existing landscape review](../reports/Adaptive%20fitness%20coaching%20app%20landscape.md).

Method: purposive primary-source documentation and policy review, plus peer-reviewed validation literature; not a systematic review or a live vendor benchmark. No paid API calls or meal-photo uploads were made. **Documented capability** means documented interface/behavior, not verified performance. **Vendor claim** means an unvalidated marketing/performance assertion. **Unknown** means not established from the sources reviewed. All architecture, evaluation and selection suggestions below are **research recommendations/inferences**, not adopted product decisions. URLs are direct sources; pricing is a point-in-time public-page snapshot, excluding tax, hosting and retries unless stated.

## Answer in brief

The clearest low-volume option to evaluate is **a general multimodal model for identification/quantity extraction, followed by USDA FoodData Central (FDC) lookup and deterministic nutrient calculation**. This separates uncertain perception from auditable nutrient data, while FDC's CC0/public-domain terms allow durable on-device nutrient snapshots. It does **not** make portion estimates accurate. General models support image/text structured extraction; FDC supplies search/detail records rather than recognition. Sources: [OpenAI model](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-5.6-terra), [structured output](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/structured-outputs), [FDC API and licence](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/api-guide.html), [independent LLM validation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12513282/).

Dedicated providers can bundle recognition, matching and portions, but are not necessarily a different kind of perception engine: **Edamam explicitly uses LLM vision**, and Passio documents multiple underlying model providers. Their advantage is integration and food-data plumbing, not demonstrated superiority on this user's meals. Commercial persistence rights and image retention are decision gates, not paperwork to defer. Sources: [Edamam](https://developer.edamam.com/food-database-api), [Passio model routing/privacy](https://passio.gitbook.io/nutrition-ai/rest-api/api-overview/zero-data-retention), [fatsecret storable data](https://platform.fatsecret.com/docs/guides/storable-data), [LogMeal privacy](https://logmeal.com/privacy/).

## 4. Retention, training and stateless proxy feasibility

**Three different properties:** (1) proxy does not persist meals, (2) vendor does not persist meals, (3) vendor does not train on meals. None implies the others. The app must persist its offline draft somewhere locally; a stateless cloud proxy cannot process a queued capture while the phone never submits it. This is an architectural inference, not a vendor guarantee.

**What remains unknown:** No independent, current, head-to-head validation of the exact Passio, Edamam Vision, fatsecret v2 and LogMeal configurations on this user's meal distribution was established. Vendor category precision, search relevance, nutrient-table quality, image classification probability and kcal error are different measures. The evidence above supports **editable drafts with explicit assumptions**, not autonomous precise nutrition logging. No peer-reviewed result here calibrates a returned “confidence 0.9” to 90% probability of correct meal nutrients. This is an evidence-gap conclusion, not proof such research does not exist.

## 6. Proposed draft/proxy contract and evaluation

These are **proposals for later implementation**, not code or an adopted architecture. They operationalize the ticket brief and the error patterns in [the 2025 validation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12513282/) and [2023 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10836267/).

1. **Save capture first:** local draft ID, capture time/day, input type, local photo or sentence, revision, processing state (`queued`, `recognizing`, `needs-review`, `failed`). App owns queue and retry/backoff; sending later does not change original meal time. A failure leaves the capture intact. Do not introduce barcode/manual-macro/saved-meal fallbacks.
2. **One request contains what recognition needs:** strip EXIF/location, resize appropriately, send only food input and optional locale. Proxy authenticates, limits size and spend, and keeps secrets server-side. Do not forward HealthKit history, goals or identifiers as recognition context. Suppress bodies from request/error/APM logs; temporary image files must not outlive request handling.
3. **Separate three uncertainties:** recognized identity; portion/grams; database match. Keep candidates, visible versus assumed ingredients, raw provider score/name, model/version, explicit/inferred quantity, database ID and nutrient basis. Label confidence as qualitative review status unless empirically calibrated; no invented precision percentages.
4. **Ground before totaling:** lookup actual records; allow null/unresolved items; distinguish raw/cooked/drained food and grams versus millilitres. Recompute from accepted quantities and stored nutrient basis. Do not double-count a composite dish plus its exploded ingredients. Missing macros are unknown, not zero.
5. **Review, do not auto-log:** user can correct food identity/portion or supply a one-sentence clarification and retry. Guessed oil, sauces and preparation remain disclosed assumptions. A failed recognizer can leave a draft queued; “no manual-macro mode” must not mean fabricate a successful result.
6. **Protect edits during retries:** associate response with draft ID/input revision; ignore stale results after an edit/delete. A client request ID does not by itself make a paid vendor request idempotent. Stateless proxies cannot guarantee exactly-once billing after lost responses without some durable coordination; assess whether bounded duplicate request cost is acceptable for personal use. Local revision checks can still prevent duplicate diary entries.
7. **Only retain licensed snapshots:** Food Draft/log persistence, exports and local offline totals must be permitted by selected database contract. A stateless proxy is compatible with a read-only shared nutrient catalogue/cache, but the strictest no-persistence variant can query FDC on demand and retain accepted public-domain snapshots on device.

**Proposed evaluation before choosing a vendor/model:** use the same 30–50 representative meals in photo and one-sentence form, deliberately including mixed dishes, large portions, oils/sauces, drinks and the user's regional foods. Obtain weighed ingredient/portion references and document database reference assumptions. Compare initial and user-corrected results separately, repeat a subset, and pin model/configuration. This is a pragmatic personal-use pilot, not statistically powered clinical validation.

- Report item precision/recall, correct preparation/database match, portion absolute/relative error, kcal and protein MAE plus signed bias, failure/unknown rate and correction burden.
- Report capture-to-draft and capture-to-confirmed p50/p95, including upload, lookup and retry; token counts, effective per-capture cost and monthly minimum separately.
- Check whether “high confidence” cases actually have lower error; keep any reliability threshold explicit and exploratory.
- Exercise offline capture, restart, batch backlog, timeout, rate limit, lost response, stale response after edit and delete-before-retry.
- Verify contracted persistence/export rights, no-training policy and deletion behavior before uploading private benchmark meals. Set acceptance thresholds with the owner; none are silently imposed by this report.

## Recommendation for the decision

**Options, not a final decision:**

1. **General vision/text model + USDA FDC** — strongest starting candidate for low-volume personal evaluation: transparent pay-per-token spend, CC0 durable nutrient snapshots and one extraction strategy for both inputs. Trade-offs are matching/unit-conversion work, US-centred coverage and still-uncertain portions. OpenAI's current Terra is a documented candidate; Gemini is a comparative candidate only after personal-use eligibility and retention are resolved. Choose a model by meal-specific test, not benchmark reputation. Sources: [OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-5.6-terra), [Google terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms), [FDC](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/api-guide.html).
2. **Passio REST bundled extraction/grounding** — less integration work and both input types with nutrition previews. Trade-offs: $99 monthly floor, $599 tier for documented ZDR toggle, upstream model/fallback dependence and snapshot/licence questions, including OFF obligations. Worth a trial if developer effort matters more than personal-scale cost. Sources: [plans](https://passio.gitbook.io/nutrition-ai/getting-started/token-system-and-pricing), [ZDR](https://passio.gitbook.io/nutrition-ai/rest-api/api-overview/zero-data-retention), [OFF terms](https://passio.gitbook.io/nutrition-ai/rest-api/api-overview/open-food-facts-terms-of-use).
3. **Edamam Vision/NLP or fatsecret image/NLP** — alternatives when curated generic/restaurant foods or country-specific data reduce correction burden. Trade-offs: commercial monthly/add-on fees and default storage restrictions; require written durable offline quantity/nutrient/export rights and API-specific retention/no-training terms before adopting either. Edamam Core is not automatically permission for a full total-carbohydrate offline diary; fatsecret free database access is not free recognition. Sources: [Edamam plans/licence](https://developer.edamam.com/food-database-api), [fatsecret editions](https://platform.fatsecret.com/api-editions), [fatsecret storage](https://platform.fatsecret.com/docs/guides/storable-data).
4. **LogMeal specialized photo path** — useful if segmentation, dish candidates and later depth capture prove valuable. Trade-offs: provider-side intakes/history, default indefinite retention/model-development use, quantity-plan/capture ambiguity and an additional text parser. It is a weaker fit for this exact photo-or-sentence, stateless-data-minimization brief unless terms and workflow are changed. Sources: [recognition](https://docs.logmeal.com/docs/guides-features-several-dishes-recognition), [quantity](https://docs.logmeal.com/docs/guides-features-quantity-estimation), [privacy](https://logmeal.com/privacy/).

**Decision questions to bring back:** Which cuisines/brands must work? Is bounded provider retention acceptable, or is contractual ZDR mandatory? Must nutrient snapshots survive cancellation and export? What observed correction time, energy/protein error and monthly spend are acceptable? The answers plus a small paired benchmark should choose among these options; no provider is selected by closing this research ticket.
