# Strength-Workout Logging & Adaptive Training Apps (Strong, Hevy, Fitbod, JEFIT, Alpha Progression, Ladder, Freeletics)

**Scope / method.** All facts below were taken on **1 November 2026** from (a) official Apple App Store product pages (developer-authored descriptions and Apple's IAP price lists), (b) official product/marketing sites, and (c) official help-center articles. Where a capability is asserted only in marketing copy, it is labelled as such. Access date for every URL: **2026-11-01**. No independent benchmark, audit, or peer-reviewed evaluation of any app's adaptive algorithm was found.

**Binding caveat for the whole memo:** every adaptive/AI claim in this category is a vendor claim. None of the seven vendors publishes validation data, model documentation, or outcome studies. The single most important distinction in this landscape is therefore **documented mechanism** (a help-center page describing inputs and update cadence) versus **marketing label** ("AI", "adaptive", "smart").

## Q1. How are sets, reps and weights logged; are previous-session values prefilled; are rest timers, routines and progress charts available?

### Takeaway
The "fast logging" problem is already solved to a high standard by Strong, Hevy, JEFIT and Alpha Progression: all four are per-set, prefill-or-recommend, auto-rest-timer products, and Alpha Progression has pushed the interaction down to two taps plus a Live Activity. Freeletics is the outlier — it is a session/feedback product, not a set-table logger. Prefill is explicitly documented everywhere except Strong and Ladder.

### Cited Findings
- **Strong** logs each exercise as one or more sets, with weight and reps entered per set and a checkbox to complete it; it provides an auto rest timer, templates, supersets, set types (warm-up/failure/drop), and an exercise library — [Strong Help: How do I perform a workout](https://help.strongapp.io/article/229-my-first-workout).
- Strong's own feature list claims "Advanced Statistics… personal records and progression", 1RM and total-volume calculation, "graphs for Volume and 1RM Progression", a "built in Auto Countdown Timer", a warm-up calculator and plate calculator — [App Store listing, Strong](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id464254577).
- **Strong's prefill of last session's numbers is not documented** in either the App Store description or the workout help article; a search of Strong's help center for "last time" returns 40 articles but none describing previous-value prefill — [Strong Help search](https://help.strongapp.io/search?query=last%20time).
- **Hevy** explicitly states: "See last session's weights and reps for every exercise, right where you need them. No guessing, no scrolling back through history," and "Log weights, reps, duration, and RPE in seconds without breaking the flow of your sets," with "automatic rest timers between sets" and per-exercise strength graphs — [App Store listing, Hevy](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1458862350).
- **JEFIT** documents prefill as a core feature: "Every set is pre-filled with what you did last time," the rest timer "starts automatically after each set and counts down out loud," sets show estimated 1RM and PR alerts, and finishing captures one RPE rating for the whole session — [JEFIT: Log & Track](https://www.jefit.com/features/log-track).
- JEFIT lets the user choose the prefill source — "last log on any day, or the same workout day" — and keeps uncapped per-exercise history — [JEFIT: Exercise History](https://www.jefit.com/features/exercise-history).
- **Alpha Progression** logs "Weight, reps and RIR in two taps, with your notes and your last sets in view," and runs the rest timer as a Live Activity on the lock screen and in the Dynamic Island; supports supersets, dropsets, bodyweight, assisted and timed exercises, and works fully offline — [App Store listing, Alpha Progression](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1462277793).
- Alpha Progression adds a warm-up calculator (warm-up sets auto-added and scaled to the working weight) and a plate calculator that shows "the closest weight below if your plates don't add up" — [Alpha Progression pricing/features page](https://alphaprogression.com/en/pricing).
- **Fitbod** exposes sets, reps and weight fields with dedicated help documentation on how unilateral vs bilateral movements are logged, and supports logging Reps in Reserve per exercise — [Fitbod: Sets, Reps, Weight Fields](https://help.fitbod.me/hc/en-us/articles/29486697282711-Exercise-Details-Screen-Sets-Reps-and-Weight-Fields-Explained); [Fitbod: RiR](https://help.fitbod.me/hc/en-us/articles/360033133174-Reps-in-Reserve-RiR-Formerly-Exertion-Rating-RPE).
- **Ladder** documents "Progress tracking through workout logs for sets, reps, and weights to monitor improvement" and a "Ladder Journal" to "visualize your rep and weight progress over time" — [Ladder Help: What is Ladder?](https://www.joinladder.com/help-center/what-is-ladder); [Ladder pricing](https://joinladder.com/pricing). Depth of its logging UI (prefill, per-set rest) is not documented.
- **Freeletics** is not a set-table logger. Its weights programming is organised into "Weights Journeys" built on compound lifts (squat, deadlift, bench, push press, row) with 42-session plans — [Freeletics Help: Weights Journeys](https://help.freeletics.com/hc/en-us/articles/360001995859-Freeletics-Weights-Journeys). For dumbbell/kettlebell work in bodyweight journeys, "the Coach won't assign a weight" and the athlete chooses it — [Freeletics Help: What weight should I use](https://help.freeletics.com/hc/en-us/articles/360021581179-What-weight-should-I-use-for-my-Kettlebell-Dumbbell-and-Plate-exercises).
- Freeletics does support manual entry of external training ("Log an exercise, workout or run outside of your Training Journey") and a Workout Creator where the user specifies reps, duration and "the exact weight to be used for any weighted exercise" — [Freeletics Help Center index](https://help.freeletics.com/hc/en-us); [Freeletics Help: Workout Creator](https://help.freeletics.com/hc/en-us/articles/8694063527570-Create-your-own-Workout-with-the-Workout-Creator).

### Inferences
- **Minimum viable bar for Motive:** prefill (or a per-set target) + auto rest timer + per-exercise history graph is now table stakes, not differentiation. Hevy, JEFIT and Alpha Progression each market it as the headline.
- Alpha Progression's "two taps + Live Activity" is the current interaction benchmark for a weights logger on iOS; Hevy's "last session right where you need them" is the benchmark for information placement.
- Freeletics' lack of a set/rep/weight table means it does not compete for the same logging job. It competes for "tell me what to do today" adherence — a different job that Motive's coach layer will partly overlap.
- Prefill is cheap to implement but has a hidden design cost: it anchors the user to last week's load, which fights algorithmic progression. JEFIT resolves this by prefilling and letting the user "beat it, or confirm it"; Alpha Progression resolves it by prefilling a *recommendation* rather than a *record*.

### Gaps
- **Strong prefill is unverified.** Neither the App Store copy nor the help center describes previous-session values on the logging screen. Do not assert it in the final report without a screenshot-level source.
- **No first-party source quantifies logging speed** (taps per set, seconds per set). Every "fast" claim is marketing. Motive cannot claim "fastest" on evidence; it would need its own instrumented benchmark.
- Ladder's logging UI depth (prefill, per-set rest timer behaviour) is undocumented publicly.
- Whether Freeletics' Barbell/Hybrid journeys capture weight per set per exercise is not stated in any help article reviewed.

## Q2. Is planning static, template-based, algorithmically adaptive, AI-branded, or human coached? What inputs drive adaptation?

### Takeaway
The market splits into four distinct models, and the label does not predict the mechanism: **static/template** (Strong), **deterministic algorithm** (Alpha Progression, JEFIT Adaptive Plan, Hevy Trainer), **proprietary-algorithm-plus-AI-marketing** (Fitbod), **human-coach content** (Ladder), and **conversational AI coach** (Freeletics). Documented adaptation inputs are almost always *training-log-derived* — performance, recovery, equipment, schedule — and almost never *physiological* (sleep, HRV, readiness).

### Cited Findings
- **Strong** is explicitly non-prescriptive: it "doesn't bog you down with distractions" and is "optimized for a progressive barbell routine such as Starting Strength or StrongLifts 5x5, but is easily adaptable to any other routine of your choosing" — [App Store listing, Strong](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id464254577). No adaptive programming feature is documented.
- **Hevy Trainer** is "an algorithm-based feature that creates workout programs based on your fitness goals, experience, available equipment, workout frequency, and time constraints," generating "workouts, exercises, rep ranges, rest periods, recommendations for starting weights"; ongoing guidance includes "Suggested starting weights for new exercises (when you've logged the exercise at least once before)," "Automatic weight progression based on performance," and "Gentle reminders to maintain intensity if progress stalls" — [Hevy: Announcing Hevy Trainer](https://www.hevyapp.com/announcing-hevy-trainer/).
- Hevy Trainer's progress report tracks "week-to-week workout consistency, time spent training, number of completed sets," weekly volume distribution by muscle group, and whether the user is "hitting the recommended number of sets per body part," with bodyweight displayed alongside — [Hevy: Announcing Hevy Trainer](https://www.hevyapp.com/announcing-hevy-trainer/).
- **Fitbod** documents five exercise-selection inputs: "how effective an exercise is for your stated goal, what equipment you have available, how recovered each muscle group is, how much variety you've asked for, and the feedback you've given on specific exercises," with effectiveness "drawn from data across more than 87 million logged workouts" — [Fitbod: How does Fitbod choose which exercises](https://help.fitbod.me/hc/en-us/articles/43489509474455-How-does-Fitbod-choose-which-exercises-to-give-me).
- Fitbod's load prescription uses "its proprietary mStrength™ technology to vary intensity and volume between sessions"; completing an exercise comfortably increases load/reps, struggling reduces them, and "Reps in Reserve ratings and Max Effort Day results refine this further" — [Fitbod: How does Fitbod decide my sets, reps and weight?](https://help.fitbod.me/hc/en-us/articles/43489869175063-How-does-Fitbod-decide-my-sets-reps-and-weight) (page updated 15 Sep 2026).
- Fitbod's workout regenerates when training history, recovery, equipment or profile settings change — including when a cardio session is imported from Apple Health in the background, meaning "a workout can change without you doing anything in Fitbod" — [Fitbod: Why did my workout change?](https://help.fitbod.me/hc/en-us/articles/42333470514455-Why-did-my-workout-change-Understanding-workout-refreshes).
- Fitbod markets "AI-Generated Workouts," an "Adaptive AI [that] learns from your edits to its workout recommendations," and "non-linear periodization" — [App Store listing, Fitbod](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1041517543) — **marketing claim**; the help center describes rules and logged-data feedback, not a model.
- **JEFIT's Adaptive Plan** is "a 12-week training program built around your goal, schedule and equipment," running "in four-week blocks… build, peak and deload," which "re-reads your logs and adjusts every week"; inputs are "your goal, level, schedule, equipment and injuries," and it is "built on… the training data of JEFIT's 13M+ users" — [JEFIT: Adaptive Plan](https://www.jefit.com/features/adaptive-plan).
- JEFIT also markets a separate "AI-powered progressive overload system" that "analyzes your past workout logs to suggest exactly what weight and reps to lift next" — [App Store listing, JEFIT](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id449810000). Its October 2026 release notes say "Adaptive Plans now check in after week two — tell us how training's going," i.e. an explicit self-report feedback prompt — [App Store version history, JEFIT 17.3.0](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id449810000).
- **Alpha Progression** deliberately avoids AI language. Its plan generator is driven by "experience, goal, schedule, equipment, and muscle priorities," choosing "a training split, exercises, and set and rep targets," and its recommendations are "calculated from your past performance," with the algorithm pointing "to the next strength level" — [Alpha Progression pricing/features](https://alphaprogression.com/en/pricing); [Alpha Progression blog: Workout Plan Generator](https://alphaprogression.com/en/blog/alpha-progression-workout-plan-generator).
- Alpha Progression's recommendation model is explicitly per-set and fatigue-aware: targets are "based on your previous workouts and what you have already done today. Recommendations shift from set to set because fatigue is real: set 3 is not set 1" — [App Store listing, Alpha Progression](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1462277793).
- Alpha Progression weights practicality over theoretical precision: "A theoretically optimal recommendation of 31.7 kg is not useful if your machine jumps directly from 30 to 35 kg," so gym equipment and increments constrain recommendations — [Alpha Progression Guide](https://alphaprogression.com/en/blog/alpha-progression-guide).
- **Ladder** is human-coach-authored: "Structured progressive overload programs created by expert coaches," with a new plan each week and 21 named coach/program pairings (e.g. ASCEND/Aria Vince, FORGED/Corey Perkins, MAXIMUS/Bobby Maximus) — [App Store listing, Ladder](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1502936453).
- Ladder's own help center describes "professionally designed training programs and daily coach-prescribed workouts that are new each week" and "Structured weekly programs lasting 5–6 weeks focused on progressive strength gains," with no per-user algorithmic adaptation documented — [Ladder Help: What is Ladder?](https://www.joinladder.com/help-center/what-is-ladder).
- **Freeletics** is the only vendor in this set that brands a conversational AI coach: "Your AI Coach customizes each workout to your fitness level, adjusting in real time based on your feedback," with "30 fitness plans" and "1 trillion workout combinations" — [App Store listing, Freeletics](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id654810212).
- Freeletics' adaptation is documented as feedback-driven across a Training Journey: "the Coach will continuously adapt your plan based on your athlete profile, performance, and feedback after each workout" — [Freeletics Help: Choose your Training Journey](https://help.freeletics.com/hc/en-us/articles/360001805519-Choose-your-Freeletics-Training-Journey).
- Freeletics also runs a longitudinal score: the Daily Athlete Score "analyzes your training over the past three months" and "takes Coach sessions and any other logged trainings or runs into account, helping to further personalize your future Coach sessions" — [Freeletics Help: Daily Athlete Score](https://help.freeletics.com/hc/en-us/articles/14322804624146-The-Daily-Athlete-Score-DAS).
- **No vendor documents using sleep, HRV, resting heart rate, or readiness to change strength prescription.** Fitbod uses heart rate only for calorie estimation and imported cardio for recovery — [Fitbod: How Fitbod Calculates Calories](https://help.fitbod.me/hc/en-us/articles/360004826434-How-Fitbod-Calculates-Calories); [Fitbod: Why did my workout change?](https://help.fitbod.me/hc/en-us/articles/42333470514455-Why-did-my-workout-change-Understanding-workout-refreshes).

### Inferences
- **"AI" is a marketing layer, not a mechanism, in this category.** Four of the five adaptive products describe rules over logged sets; none publishes model documentation. Alpha Progression is the notable strategic counter-position: it sells the same job ("stop guessing") while explicitly refusing the AI label.
- The genuine differentiator is **adaptation granularity**: Alpha Progression adapts *per set within a session* (fatigue-aware), Fitbod adapts *exercise selection and session prescription* (recovery-aware), JEFIT and Hevy adapt *weekly load progression* (log-aware). Motive should decide which layer it competes on, because "adaptive" means different things in each.
- **The physiological-signal gap is real and unoccupied.** Apple Health/Watch sleep, HRV and resting HR are available to every one of these apps and none documents using them in strength prescription. That is Motive's clearest defensible claim — but it is also unvalidated territory, so it needs a stated, inspectable rule (e.g. "HRV below baseline → hold load, cut a set") rather than a vague "AI adapts to recovery."
- Human coaching (Ladder) beats algorithms on *program quality perception* and loses on *personalisation*; Ladder's 4.95 rating over 215K reviews versus Alpha Progression's 4.92 over 2.3K suggests brand/coach attachment drives scale more than algorithmic sophistication.
- Expect user trust to hinge on **explainability**. JEFIT's weekly check-in and Alpha Progression's visible per-set target are explainable by construction; Fitbod's background workout refreshes ("a workout can change without you doing anything") are the opposite and are a plausible churn/trust risk.

### Gaps
- **No independent validation exists** for any of the seven algorithms: no RCTs, no accuracy audits, no published adherence or strength-outcome comparisons. All efficacy claims are vendor-authored.
- Hevy Trainer's actual progression rule (increment size, stall handling, deload logic) is not published beyond the announcement post.
- JEFIT's Adaptive Plan and "AI progressive overload" are described in marketing terms; whether they are the same engine is unclear.
- Fitbod's "87 million logged workouts" effectiveness dataset is unauditable and its methodology is not described.
- Ladder's "General Strength Series" and "Ladder Flex" products are listed in its help center but their planning logic was not reviewed.

## Q3. What Apple Health, Apple Fitness and Apple Watch support is documented? Can users log from the Watch or only sync summaries?

### Takeaway
Watch-side *logging* is standard among the loggers — Strong and Hevy log full workouts wrist-only, Fitbod logs sets but depends on the iPhone to start and save, JEFIT live-syncs — while Alpha Progression has no Watch app at all. Health integration is mostly one-directional (workouts out); only Fitbod documents reading workouts and body composition back in. No app documents using Apple Fitness metrics (rings, cardio fitness, HRV) to drive strength programming.

### Cited Findings
- **Strong** ships a "Fully-featured Apple Watch app to log workouts with or without your iPhone" — [App Store listing, Strong](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id464254577).
- On Strong's Watch app the user selects a routine or empty workout, sees a Workout Overview, taps a set to open a Set Screen where "you can view and edit the values of a set (such as weight and reps)" with the Digital Crown, adds/deletes sets via long-press, changes set type and RPE, and sees heart rate "at all times in the header"; the rest timer can be edited with the Crown. Units and per-exercise rest durations cannot be changed on the watch — [Strong Help: Perform a Workout with Strong for Apple Watch](https://help.strongapp.io/article/224-workout-on-apple-watch).
- Strong "fully syncs with Apple Health," explicitly to "Get Nutrition information into and out of Strong," "Track your bodyweight and body fat %," and "View Workouts outside of Strong (including Activity Rings if using Apple Watch)" — [Strong Help: How do I sync with Apple Health?](https://help.strongapp.io/article/147-sync-with-apple-health).
- Strong's calorie figures come from Apple's own APIs: "Strong records an estimate of calorie consumption using Apple Watch's in built software and Apple APIs, and we do not control the calories sent to Apple Health directly" — [Strong Help: incorrect calories](https://help.strongapp.io/search?query=apple%20watch).
- **Hevy**'s Watch app lets users "Log sets, start rest timers, and finish your workout without touching your phone," "Monitor your heart rate during your workout," add complications, and "Workouts save automatically when your watch reconnects to your phone" — [App Store listing, Hevy](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1458862350).
- Hevy states: "Hevy uses HealthKit and CareKit to export your workouts to the Health app. You can turn this on in Settings" — [App Store listing, Hevy](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1458862350). This is **write-out only** as described; no import of Health workouts is documented.
- **Fitbod**'s Watch app "works alongside the iPhone app. Start and save workouts from your iPhone while your Apple Watch lets you log sets, track your rest timer, and monitor heart rate." On the watch a user can "Log sets and mark exercises complete," adjust "reps, weight, distance, duration, resistance, incline, or band setting per set," and add/remove sets "up to 9 sets per exercise"; the article has an explicit "What requires your iPhone" section, and Always On display is unsupported — [Fitbod: Apple Watch](https://help.fitbod.me/hc/en-us/articles/360006499194-Apple-Watch).
- Fitbod's Health integration is bidirectional: "Send logged workouts to Apple Health and import compatible Apple Health workouts into Fitbod," plus body fat percentage, weight and lean mass synced to "refine recommendations over time" — [Fitbod: Apple Health](https://help.fitbod.me/hc/en-us/articles/360047357794-Apple-Health).
- Fitbod treats imported activity as training history: importing "a cardio or activity session from a connected app such as Apple Health" updates recovery and regenerates the next workout — [Fitbod: Why did my workout change?](https://help.fitbod.me/hc/en-us/articles/42333470514455-Why-did-my-workout-change-Understanding-workout-refreshes).
- Fitbod added **AirPods-based set logging**: with AirPods Pro, "Nod, shake, or tilt your head to log sets without touching your phone," with selectable gesture and sensitivity, and AirPods Pro 3 add live heart rate (iOS 26+) — [Fitbod: Apple AirPods Pro](https://help.fitbod.me/hc/en-us/articles/360056464934-Apple-AirPod-Pros); [Fitbod: Live Heart Rate Tracking](https://help.fitbod.me/hc/en-us/articles/39224086573207-Live-Heart-Rate-Tracking).
- Fitbod notes a Health-platform mismatch: "Currently, Apple Health does not recognize Fitbod's pause feature so your Apple Watch will continue to track your activity even though you are in a paused state in Fitbod" — [Fitbod: Pause a Workout](https://help.fitbod.me/hc/en-us/articles/23376692769559-Pause-a-Workout).
- **JEFIT** documents "Live-sync your workout between JEFIT and your Apple Watch," heart rate and calories during training, and "See your set count and rest timer right on your lock screen"; its Elite plan advertises "Unlimited watch workouts," implying a free-tier watch limit — [App Store listing, JEFIT](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id449810000); [JEFIT Elite pricing](https://www.jefit.com/elite/).
- JEFIT syncs "workouts, calories, heart rate and body weight with Apple Health," configured in Settings → Connected Apps with a per-workout auto-sync toggle; sync and export are "included in every JEFIT account" — [JEFIT: Apple Health](https://www.jefit.com/features/apple-health).
- **Alpha Progression** states only "Syncs with Apple Health," covering "Workouts, including calories burned," bodyweight and body fat percentage, and the integration is optional — [App Store listing, Alpha Progression](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1462277793); [Alpha Progression Guide](https://alphaprogression.com/en/blog/alpha-progression-guide). **No Apple Watch app is mentioned anywhere** in its listing, pricing page, guide or support pages.
- **Ladder** instructs users to grant Apple Health read/write access and says the Watch "should automatically open Ladder" when a workout starts, displaying "your current movement and stats (calories and BPM)… in real time"; Apple Watch "does not sync with Ladder Web" — [Ladder Help: Apple Watch sync & troubleshooting](https://www.joinladder.com/help-center/apple-watch-sync-troubleshooting).
- Ladder's documented Health use case is **steps**: "Open Ladder → Settings → Connections → Apple Health → Toggle on Steps," with the Steps widget updating "roughly every hour" — [Ladder Help: Apple Health & wearable data](https://www.joinladder.com/help-center/apple-health-data).
- **Freeletics** states "Control your workout from your Apple Watch and track your fitness data in the Apple Health app" — [App Store listing, Freeletics](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id654810212). Its help center confirms the Watch app can "start, complete and finish your training without you having to touch your iPhone," but "Your iPhone should… be in range at all time"; supported on watchOS 8+, Series 3 and later — [Freeletics Help: How to train with the Apple Watch](https://help.freeletics.com/hc/en-us/articles/4402770222866-How-to-train-with-the-Apple-Watch).
- Freeletics syncs "all the workouts you are doing in the Training App with Apple Health" via a settings toggle, and its habit metric "Base" (17 minutes of daily movement) counts "any activity synced with Apple Health" — [Freeletics Help: Apple Health](https://help.freeletics.com/hc/en-us/articles/115004675105-Use-the-Apple-Health-feature); [Freeletics Help: What is Base?](https://help.freeletics.com/hc/en-us/articles/16558820834578-What-is-Base).

### Inferences
- **Watch logging is table stakes, not a differentiator.** Strong and Hevy already do full wrist-only logging; Fitbod adds head-gesture logging via AirPods, which is the most interesting low-input innovation in the set. Motive cannot win on "you can log from your Watch."
- **Alpha Progression's missing Watch app is a genuine, exploitable weakness** for an Apple-first entrant: its logging interaction is the best in the set but is iPhone-only.
- **Health is used as a data *sink*, not a signal source.** Six of seven write workouts to Health; only Fitbod reads workouts and body composition back. Nobody reads sleep/HRV/readiness. The Watch's richest signals (HRV, sleep stages, cardio fitness, wrist temperature) are unused across the entire competitive set.
- Freeletics' Watch app requires the iPhone in range, which makes it weaker than Strong/Hevy for gym use (phone in a locker). Worth noting as a competitor limitation.
- Fitbod's AirPods gesture logging is the clearest precedent for Motive's "extremely fast, low-input" thesis — and it is a first-party documented feature, not marketing.

### Gaps
- Whether Ladder can **log sets from the Watch** (versus display live metrics) is not documented.
- Whether Ladder writes strength workouts back to Apple Health is not documented; only steps import is described.
- Alpha Progression's App Store "Compatibility" section was not captured; absence of Watch documentation is strong but not conclusive evidence that no Watch app exists.
- JEFIT's free-tier watch limit ("Unlimited watch workouts" as an Elite perk) is not quantified.
- No vendor documents Apple Fitness+ or Activity Ring integration beyond Strong's mention of viewing Activity Rings.

## Q4. Is nutrition tracking included, and if so at what depth?

### Takeaway
Nutrition is the weakest capability in this competitive set and the clearest opening for Motive. Five of seven apps have no food logging; Strong only *displays* macros imported from other apps; Freeletics sells nutrition as a separate, deliberately non-calorie-counting app; only Ladder ships real food logging, and it is AI-photo/voice based — the current bar for low-input capture.

### Cited Findings
- **Strong** does not log food. It displays nutrition imported from Apple Health or Google Fit and lets users "view your nutrition information and goals on Strong's Profile Widgets"; macronutrients come "from any Apple Health compatible nutrition app" (FoodNoms, Lifesum, MyFitnessPal, Lose it), and total calories can be typed in manually via "Measurement > Caloric Intake" — [Strong Help: Track Nutrition with Strong](https://help.strongapp.io/article/240-track-nutrition-with-strong).
- **Hevy**, **Fitbod**, **JEFIT** and **Alpha Progression** list no nutrition or food-logging capability in their App Store descriptions or feature pages — [Hevy](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1458862350); [Fitbod](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1041517543); [JEFIT](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id449810000); [Alpha Progression](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1462277793).
- Fitbod's own help-center search for "nutrition" returns no food-logging article; it returns Fitness Goals and Ask a Trainer instead, indicating no nutrition feature — [Fitbod Help search](https://fitbod.zendesk.com/api/v2/help_center/articles/search.json?query=nutrition).
- **Ladder** ships Ladder Nutrition with three logging routes: "Smart Log — describe, photograph, or voice-dictate what you ate for an AI-powered calorie estimate," database "Search," and "Barcode Scan," all feeding a reviewable "food cart"; Smart Log accepts typed description, voice dictation and "one or multiple photos… no limit," and asks "a few multiple-choice clarifying questions" when the AI needs context — [Ladder Help: Tracking Your Macros with Smart Log](https://www.joinladder.com/help-center/tracking-macros-smart-log) (updated 6 Oct 2026).
- Ladder's Smart Log "Requires iOS 18 or later. Available on iPhone only" — [Ladder Help: Tracking Your Macros with Smart Log](https://www.joinladder.com/help-center/tracking-macros-smart-log).
- Ladder's help center lists a Nutrition topic with five articles (Getting Started, Recipes, Tracking Macros with Smart Log), confirming nutrition is a maintained product area rather than a stub — [Ladder Help Center](https://www.joinladder.com/help-center).
- **Freeletics Nutrition** is a separate app and coach, priced at "$34.99 for 3 months and $79.99 for 12 months," offering "more than 330 easy recipes," "individualized nutrition plans," a weekly shopping list, and explicitly "No calorie counting. Only one measurement: Fast and visible results" — [App Store listing, Freeletics Nutrition](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1082670451).
- Freeletics Nutrition adapts portions to training: "Did you train? Log your training so the Coach can adapt your portion sizes" — [App Store listing, Freeletics Nutrition](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1082670451). The main Freeletics app sells a "Training & Nutrition Bundle" as a subscription option — [App Store listing, Freeletics](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id654810212).

### Inferences
- **Nutrition is unoccupied territory among serious strength loggers.** A user who wants fast set logging *and* food logging currently has to run two apps (e.g. Hevy + MacroFactor) or accept Ladder's coach-led program. Motive's combined logging + nutrition is a genuine wedge, not a me-too feature.
- **Ladder's Smart Log defines the UX bar**: one input surface accepting text, voice and unlimited photos, with AI clarifying questions and a review step before saving. Any Motive nutrition design that starts with a food database search will feel a generation behind.
- Freeletics' anti-calorie-counting stance is a deliberate segment choice (habit/relationship-with-food), not a capability gap. It implies the market tolerates two nutrition philosophies — precision tracking vs. coached portions — and Motive must pick one.
- Strong's pattern (display macros imported from Health) is the cheapest possible "nutrition support" and is demonstrably not a real feature; it is a widget, not a coaching loop.

### Gaps
- **JEFIT's nutrition status is unresolved.** JEFIT historically offered nutrition tracking, but no current first-party page reviewed mentions it. Mark unknown; verify against the live app before publishing.
- No vendor publishes food-database size, barcode coverage, or micronutrient depth, so "depth" can only be judged by logging modality, not by data quality.
- Ladder Nutrition's pricing is not separable from the $29.99/mo membership in any first-party source reviewed; whether it is included or an add-on is unclear.
- No independent accuracy data exists for Ladder's AI calorie estimates.

## Q5. What are the main UX strengths, limitations, pricing/paywall facts, and evidence gaps for a low-input daily coaching loop?

### Takeaway
Pricing clusters tightly ($2.99–$12.99/mo entry, ~$24–$80/yr), and the paywall line is consistently drawn at *guidance*, not *logging* — every logger gives away unlimited logging and charges for programming, charts or advanced analytics. UX strengths are speed and prefill; the shared limitations are iPhone-tethered coaching, one-directional Health sync, no physiological adaptation, and zero published evidence for any coaching claim.

### Cited Findings
- **Strong pricing:** free saves unlimited workouts but is "limited to 3 custom routines"; Strong PRO is "$4.99/month or $29.99/year" — [App Store listing, Strong](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id464254577). Apple's IAP list also shows $99.99, $79.99, $23.99, $19.99 and $3.99 entries whose products are unlabelled — [App Store, Strong](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id464254577).
- **Hevy pricing:** IAP list shows "Hevy Pro - Monthly $2.99," "Hevy Pro - Yearly $23.99," "Hevy Pro - Lifetime $74.99," plus a second monthly tier at $3.99 — [App Store, Hevy](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1458862350). Free tier keeps logging but Pro adds "Unlimited routines and custom exercises" and "Your complete training history," plus Hevy Trainer — [App Store listing, Hevy](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1458862350).
- **Fitbod pricing:** a seven-day trial that "will automatically roll over into a paid subscription unless canceled," with monthly or yearly plans — [Fitbod Help: How the Trial Works](https://help.fitbod.me/hc/en-us/articles/30542136101527-How-the-Trial-Works). Apple's IAP list shows monthly at $9.99 and $15.99 and annual at $59.99, $79.99, $95.99 and $49.99, most marked "(Legacy Pricing)," plus a $7.99 entry — [App Store, Fitbod](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1041517543). **Current headline price cannot be stated confidently.**
- **JEFIT pricing:** Elite "$12.99/month"; Elite Annual "$69.99… $52.49 for one year, then $69.99/year," described as a "60% discount when paid annually"; Basic is free with "Free and custom workout routines," 1,400+ exercises, logging/history and community — [JEFIT Elite Membership Plans](https://www.jefit.com/elite/). Elite adds "Professionally designed workout plans," "Advanced analytics and tracking," "Unlimited watch workouts" — same source. The Adaptive Plan is Elite-gated — [JEFIT: Adaptive Plan](https://www.jefit.com/features/adaptive-plan).
- **Alpha Progression pricing:** Yearly "$79.99/year… 14-day free trial," Monthly "$12.99/month"; free tier includes "unlimited workout logging," all 795 exercise videos, own plans, measurements, rest timer, records, achievements, plan sharing and CSV export, with "No ads. No account required." Pro adds plan/workout generator, progression recommendations, charts, multiple gyms, periodization/deloads, plate and warm-up calculators — [Alpha Progression pricing](https://alphaprogression.com/en/pricing); [App Store listing, Alpha Progression](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1462277793).
- **Ladder pricing:** 7-day free trial with "NO PAYMENT collected during trial"; Pro monthly "$29.99/month"; Pro annual "$14.99/month ($179.99 for the year)"; monthly plan limits team changes to 2×/month while annual allows unlimited — [Ladder Pricing](https://joinladder.com/pricing).
- **Freeletics pricing:** six auto-renewing options — Training and Training & Nutrition, each at 3/6/12 months — [App Store listing, Freeletics](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id654810212). Apple's IAP list shows Training Coach at $34.99, $59.99, $74.99 and $79.99 and bundles at $49.99 and $89.99 without visible term lengths — [App Store, Freeletics](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id654810212). Nutrition standalone is $34.99/3 months and $79.99/12 months — [App Store, Freeletics Nutrition](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1082670451). A 14-day money-back guarantee applies — [App Store listing, Freeletics](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id654810212).
- **UX strength — speed and information placement:** Hevy's "log… in seconds without breaking the flow of your sets" with last session inline; Alpha Progression's two-tap logging with a Live Activity rest timer; JEFIT's auto-starting rest timer and prefilled sets — [Hevy](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1458862350); [Alpha Progression](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1462277793); [JEFIT](https://www.jefit.com/features/log-track).
- **UX strength — zero-friction onboarding:** Alpha Progression works without an account ("No account required") and fully offline; JEFIT and Strong both offer usable free tiers with unlimited logging — [Alpha Progression pricing](https://alphaprogression.com/en/pricing); [JEFIT Elite](https://www.jefit.com/elite/); [Strong](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id464254577).
- **UX strength — coaching without a screen:** Ladder's "in-ear coaching and video demos" with "precise pacing for every movement," and Freeletics' phone-free Watch control — [Ladder Pricing](https://joinladder.com/pricing); [Freeletics Help: Apple Watch](https://help.freeletics.com/hc/en-us/articles/4402770222866-How-to-train-with-the-Apple-Watch).
- **Limitation — adaptation is opaque and unexplained to users:** Fitbod regenerates workouts in the background when an import arrives; no vendor exposes why a load changed beyond generic prose — [Fitbod: Why did my workout change?](https://help.fitbod.me/hc/en-us/articles/42333470514455-Why-did-my-workout-change-Understanding-workout-refreshes); [Fitbod: How does Fitbod decide my sets, reps and weight?](https://help.fitbod.me/hc/en-us/articles/43489869175063-How-does-Fitbod-decide-my-sets-reps-and-weight).
- **Limitation — device/feature gating:** Fitbod requires iOS 18 and watchOS 10+ and does not support iPad; Ladder's Smart Log is iPhone-only and iOS 18+; Freeletics' Watch app needs the iPhone in range; Strong cannot change units or per-exercise rest duration from the Watch — [Fitbod: Apple Watch](https://help.fitbod.me/hc/en-us/articles/360006499194-Apple-Watch); [Fitbod: System Requirements](https://help.fitbod.me/hc/en-us/articles/38144798013719-System-Requirements); [Ladder: Smart Log](https://www.joinladder.com/help-center/tracking-macros-smart-log); [Strong: Apple Watch](https://help.strongapp.io/article/224-workout-on-apple-watch).
- **Limitation — human coaching does not scale into the app:** Ladder's personalisation is coach-authored programming plus team chat; algorithmic per-user load adjustment is not documented — [Ladder Help: What is Ladder?](https://www.joinladder.com/help-center/what-is-ladder).
- **Scale signals (vendor-reported, unaudited):** Hevy "15M+ lifters… 112M+ workouts logged in 2025"; JEFIT "12+ million people" (adaptive-plan page says "13M+ users"); Alpha Progression "25 million workouts logged" and "40,000+ lifters"; Fitbod "87 million logged workouts" — [Hevy](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1458862350); [JEFIT](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id449810000); [Alpha Progression](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1462277793); [Fitbod](https://help.fitbod.me/hc/en-us/articles/43489509474455-How-does-Fitbod-choose-which-exercises-to-give-me).
- **Awards/validation signals (editorial, not empirical):** Ladder is "Apple's 2025 App of the Year Finalist," "Apple's Editors Choice Award," "Women's Health 2026 Best Overall," "CNET 2026 Best Strength Training App"; Fitbod is an "Apple Editor's Choice Award Winner"; Alpha Progression is "App of the Day on the App Store, twice" — [Ladder](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1502936453); [Fitbod](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1041517543); [Alpha Progression](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1462277793).

### Inferences
- **Paywall architecture is settled and Motive should follow it:** free unlimited logging (acquisition) + paid guidance (monetisation). Charging for logging would be a strategic error given Strong, JEFIT and Alpha Progression all give it away. Alpha Progression's "no account required" free tier is the strongest onboarding posture in the set and is worth copying.
- **Price ceiling for a logger is ~$30/yr; for a coach it is ~$180/yr.** Hevy at $23.99/yr and Strong at $29.99/yr anchor the logging utility; Ladder at $179.99/yr and Freeletics at $34.99–$89.99 per term anchor coaching. Motive's combined logging+nutrition+coaching sits in the coaching band, which means it must justify Ladder/Fitbod-level pricing with visible coaching output, not feature count.
- **Ladder is the most dangerous competitor for Motive's target user** (high-intent, wants to be told what to do, willing to pay $180/yr, 4.95 rating over 215K reviews, now with AI nutrition) — and its weak point is exactly where Motive plans to play: no documented per-set algorithmic adaptation and no physiological-signal use.
- **The evidence gap is uniform and therefore an opportunity.** No competitor can cite an outcome study. A Motive that publishes even a modest internal validation (e.g. adherence and load-progression rates for users vs. a hold-load control) would own the only empirical claim in the category.
- **"Low-input daily loop" is currently served by two different interaction paradigms**: (1) prefill-and-confirm inside a workout (Hevy, JEFIT, Strong, AP), and (2) coach-tells-you-and-you-follow (Ladder, Freeletics). Motive's thesis — fast logging *plus* adaptive coaching *plus* nutrition *plus* Health signals — has no direct incumbent; the closest composite is Ladder (coaching + nutrition, weak adaptation) plus Fitbod (adaptation, no nutrition, no human coach).
- Digital-gym risk is real: Ladder's coach/team/chat model creates social lock-in that a purely algorithmic coach cannot replicate quickly. Motive should not compete on community.

### Gaps
- **Fitbod's current list price is genuinely unclear** because Apple's IAP list mixes "Legacy Pricing" tiers; treat any single number as unverified.
- **Freeletics' per-term pricing is unclear** — Apple's IAP list gives amounts without durations, and Freeletics' own pricing pages returned 404 on the URLs tried.
- No retention, churn, trial-conversion, or DAU/MAU data exists publicly for any of the seven.
- No time-to-log benchmark (seconds or taps per set) exists for any app; all speed claims are marketing.
- App Store ratings are self-selected and not comparable across very different review volumes (2.3K vs 288.8K).
- No evidence that any app's adaptive programming improves strength, adherence, or retention versus a static template. This is the single largest evidence gap in the landscape and should be stated as such in the final report.
